Where the energy goes

Not every appliance behaves the same way. Some draw a large amount of power for a short time, while others draw very little but never switch off.

🌡

Heating and cooling

Reverse-cycle air conditioners and heaters are the largest single category in most homes. Their usage is seasonal, so a yearly average can hide a very expensive summer or winter.

📺

Entertainment and standby

Televisions, consoles and set-top boxes use moderate power when running, but many continue to draw a small standby load around the clock. Small loads add up over a full year.

🧊

Always-on appliances

Refrigerators and freezers run continuously. Because they never switch off, even a modest improvement in efficiency produces a saving you keep collecting every single day.

How to read an energy figure

Watts measure power

A watt rating tells you how hard an appliance draws while it is switched on. On its own it says nothing about cost, because it does not account for how long the appliance runs.

Kilowatt-hours measure energy

Multiply power by time and you get energy. A 1,000 W appliance running for one hour uses one kilowatt-hour. This is the unit your retailer actually bills you for.

Tariffs turn energy into cost

Your tariff sets the price per kilowatt-hour. Multiply your kilowatt-hours by that rate to estimate what an appliance contributes to the bill.
