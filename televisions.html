Televisions

Screen size and panel technology drive most of the difference in television energy use. A larger panel lights more area, so it draws more power to reach the same brightness.

What drives a television's power draw

📐

Screen size

Power draw rises with panel area rather than with the diagonal measurement. Moving from a 55-inch to a 75-inch screen increases the lit area considerably, and consumption follows.

💡

Panel technology

LED, OLED and QLED panels produce light differently. OLED pixels emit their own light, so consumption depends heavily on how bright the on-screen content actually is.

⚙

Picture settings

Brightness, contrast and picture mode have a direct effect. A showroom or vivid preset can draw noticeably more power than the standard or eco preset on the same television.

Reducing television running costs

  • Switch the picture preset from vivid to standard or eco, which usually costs little in perceived quality.
  • Turn on the ambient light sensor so the panel dims automatically in a darker room.
  • Set a sleep timer if the television is often left running in an empty room.
  • Use a switched power board to cut standby draw from the television and connected devices together.
  • Compare the kilowatt-hour figure on the energy rating label when buying, not just the star count.
